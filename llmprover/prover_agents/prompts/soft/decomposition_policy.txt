DECOMPOSITION POLICY

Construct the parent SCRIPT first.

While building the parent proof, introduce a helper when a genuinely useful
intermediate fact would make the remaining proof substantially simpler.

After the SCRIPT is complete, output HELPERS with the statements of the newly
introduced helpers, as specified by the OUTPUT PROTOCOL.

Each helper statement should capture the fact that the parent SCRIPT actually
needs.

Prefer a small, clean decomposition that makes the parent proof substantially
simpler once the helpers are available.

A helper may itself remain nontrivial; the orchestrator may decompose it later.

Prefer genuine mathematical intermediates over helpers that only replace routine
authorized automation. Avoid introducing helpers that add little proof value.

Helper names should be fresh, short, simple Rocq identifiers.

You may reuse or improve a useful historical decomposition when it remains
relevant, but a fresh decomposition is also acceptable.

The parent proof should exploit the helpers rather than re-proving their
substantial content inline.

Avoid speculative or unused helpers.
