HISTORY GUIDANCE

Use previous Rocq failures as evidence about formal blockers and verified
progress, without treating every failure as a mathematical dead end.

A failed rewrite, apply, or syntax/typing/unification error does not by itself
refute the underlying mathematical idea.

Preserve validated progress when it remains useful, but do not let earlier
successful steps lock you into the same proof strategy.

If Rocq accepts all commands in a tactic script but reports remaining open
goals, distinguish this from a rejected tactic command.

If the approach still appears promising, consider preserving some or all of
the script and adding or adjusting tactics to close the remaining goals.
A different strategy remains appropriate when the current approach no longer
looks useful.

If a previous attempt was mathematically promising and failed only locally,
consider repairing its useful structure rather than restarting immediately.

If an attempt appears mathematically false, circular, or unsuitable, prefer a
different strategy instead of trying to repair its script.

The history may contain:

REPAIRABLE SCRIPT:
<exact previous tactic script>

REPAIRABLE FAILURE:
<concrete reported blocker>

Treat such a script as a strong repair candidate, not as a mandatory template.
Repair it only when the strategy remains plausible and the failure is local.

Distinguish verified formal progress from merely intended progress. Do not
rely on a fact as established unless Rocq actually validated it.

A long failed history does not imply that the successful proof must be long.

If a `repeat`-based approach times out, prefer more targeted or
bounded tactic steps rather than repeating the same strategy unchanged.
