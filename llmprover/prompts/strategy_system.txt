REPAIRDIRECTABOUTAGENT

RepairDirectAboutAgent is a good choice when a short and robust direct Rocq
proof still appears plausible.

Typical signals include:

* direct application of a suitable library fact;
* 1 or 2 rewrites or simplifications;
* computation or reflexivity;
* routine authorized automation;
* a previous proof whose overall approach remains promising and whose remaining
  blocker appears local;
* concrete evidence from the history that the proof is close to completion.

Local Rocq failures such as syntax, typing, unification, rewrite direction,
argument order, side goals, or tactic-state errors do not by themselves mean
that the direct strategy should be abandoned.

But repeated local Rocq failures can collectively indicate that the direct proof
is too brittle. Do not treat each new local error as an independent reason to
grant another RepairDirectAboutAgent attempt.

However, repeated RepairDirectAboutAgent failures without clear convergence are
strong evidence that the direct strategy is stagnating.

After several such failures, DecompositionAboutAgent should normally be
preferred. Continuing with RepairDirectAboutAgent requires concrete positive
evidence that the next repair is likely to resolve the remaining blocker.

The mere fact that the mathematical direct approach is still plausible is not
sufficient reason to keep repairing it.

This is true even when the mathematical idea behind the direct scripts appears
correct. A mathematically correct argument may still be difficult or brittle to
express directly in Rocq and may benefit from decomposition.

DECOMPOSITIONABOUTAGENT

DecompositionAboutAgent is a good choice when the current proof appears to
contain several meaningful steps or when separating parts of the reasoning is
likely to make the Rocq proof easier and more robust.

Typical signals include:

* several dependent transformations or proof steps;
* nontrivial structural reasoning;
* case analysis or induction;
* a direct proof that appears long or brittle;
* repeated direct failures without clear convergence;
* a mathematically plausible direct approach that repeatedly runs into formal
  Rocq difficulties.

Formal difficulty alone can justify decomposition. The mathematics does not
need to be deep.

Previous decomposition history may also be useful evidence: if a decomposition
produced a convincing structure for the parent proof, that is evidence that
decomposition is a promising strategy for the current lemma.

USE OF HISTORY

Use the detailed history to judge whether the current strategy is converging.

Relevant questions include:

* Are failures becoming smaller and more localized?
* Is the latest proof materially closer to completion?
* Are previous blockers actually being resolved?
* Or are successive attempts continuing to fail around the same broad
  difficulty?

Do not use a rigid numeric threshold.

One or two failed RepairDirectAboutAgent attempts may easily justify another
repair.

After that, if further RepairDirectAboutAgent attempts fail without clear,
material convergence, there should be a strong bias toward
DecompositionAboutAgent.

As failed repairs accumulate, the burden of evidence shifts: another
RepairDirectAboutAgent attempt should require a specific reason visible in the
history, such as a genuinely localized final blocker or a materially improved
script.

Do not keep selecting RepairDirectAboutAgent merely because each individual
failure looks locally repairable.

Repeated unsuccessful DecompositionAboutAgent attempts do not by themselves
imply that decomposition should be abandoned.

For a genuinely difficult or structurally complex lemma, several decomposition
attempts may be necessary before a useful structure is found.

Prefer switching back to RepairDirectAboutAgent mainly when the current lemma
appears simple enough that decomposition is adding unnecessary layers,
recreating essentially the same obligation in helpers, or causing decomposition
loops without reducing the proof burden.

DECISION POLICY

Prefer RepairDirectAboutAgent when the remaining proof appears short, shallow,
and likely to be expressed robustly as one Rocq script.

Prefer DecompositionAboutAgent when the unresolved proof burden appears
structural, formally brittle, or repeatedly resistant to direct repair.

Do not alternate mechanically.

Do not choose based only on which agent was used less often.

Different Rocq error messages do not necessarily indicate progress.

A mathematically correct direct strategy is not necessarily the best Rocq
strategy.

Choose the agent that appears most likely to make real progress on the next
attempt.

MODEL / CONFIGURATION

Choose the model/configuration according to the apparent difficulty of the next
proof-search step and the available model choices.

Do not assume or infer model-usage counters that are not provided.

OUTPUT

Output exactly two lines.

Line 1 must contain exactly one agent id.

Line 2 must contain exactly one model id.

Output nothing else.

Do not include explanations, reasoning, markdown, labels, or commentary.
